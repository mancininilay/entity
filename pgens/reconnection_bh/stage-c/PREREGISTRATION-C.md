# Pre-registration of the checks of stage C: R-mid and the ensemble (Claude 1, local Claude Code, 2026-11-22)

Written before any run of stage C. Brief: `2026-11-22-claude1-local-brief-bh-reconnection-stage-c.md`. It builds on the pre-registration of stage B (`2026-11-21-claude1-bh-reconnection-shakedown-preregistration.md`, SHA-256 `111e726748b26a23cb0263c3798fa88312989a72e6802348b89e89b8a1179020`), which is not changed and stays in force for checks 1, 2, 4 and 5. The same text is committed in the fork as `pgens/reconnection_bh/stage-c/PREREGISTRATION-C.md` on the local branch `dev/bh-reconnection-c` (from 97d78619, which Codex 1 is refereeing and which is not touched), next to the inputs and the new scripts; the commit and the hashes are in the report. Nothing here is changed after a run: a failure is kept and reported, and no tolerance is touched. Every verdict will be the author's, not refereed.

## 0. What is run, and what is fixed

**Executable:** the one of stage B, unchanged (commit 97d78619, `build-7520701`, SHA-256 `c7cf0f238ef3c392f8cdeae32d6f0f55a1af5474e92ee11e4dd8937a88893de8`). No engine file and no generator file changes; only inputs.

**Five runs**, inputs derived from `r-low.toml` by `stage-c/derive_inputs_c.py` (listed edits, each matching once):

| Run | Kind | Differs from R-low (R-off for the two without the policy) in |
|---|---|---|
| R-mid | policy, ten times the optical depth | `optical_depth` 0.14333; capacities; no checkpoints |
| R-low-b | policy | `cs_overdensity` 2.997 and `optical_depth` 0.014323; no ion output; capacities; no checkpoints |
| R-low-c | policy | `cs_overdensity` 3.003 and `optical_depth` 0.014343; no ion output; capacities; no checkpoints |
| R-off-b | no policy | `cs_overdensity` 2.997; no ion output; capacities; no checkpoints |
| R-off-c | no policy | `cs_overdensity` 3.003; no ion output; capacities; no checkpoints |

"Capacities": `maxnpart` of the two primary species 1.6e8 instead of 1.2e8 (the leptons of the pairs keep 4e7). "No checkpoints": `[checkpoint] keep = 0` (fact: `src/output/checkpoint.cpp:31`, `m_enabled = keep != 0`). "No ion output": `output.particles.species = [3, 4]` (the two lepton species; empty in the runs without the policy). Fields and spectra as in stage B.

**What makes the four members new realisations, and why it is innocuous.**

- **The change, for all four (my choice; the brief asks for the smallest innocuous change without touching the engine): the overdensity of the initial current layer in its fourth digit**, 3.0 to 2.997 (members b) and 3.003 (members c). The generator derives the layer from it (`pgens/reconnection_bh/pgen.hpp:297-316`): the number of layer particles per cell (4 x overdensity x profile pairs, rounded up or down at random), their temperature (sigma / (2 overdensity) = 16.7) and their drift. A change of one part in a thousand therefore (i) gives one pair more or fewer in a little under one percent of the cells of the layer at t = 0 (about 4000 pairs of hot particles: 4 x 0.003 x the integral of the profile over the 4096 x 164 cells of the layer), and (ii) changes the outcome of the rejection step of the relativistic Maxwellian (`src/archetypes/energy_dist.h:109-126`, the loop on `randeta`) for a small fraction of the particles, after which the random generator concerned is out of step for everything it serves later: the rest of the layer, the injector, and in the runs with the policy the emission. Physical effect: an initial sheet that is 0.1 percent denser and colder, in pressure balance by construction; the developed layer does not remember it.
- **R-low-b and R-low-c also carry the change of the brief: the optical depth in its fourth digit** (0.014323 and 0.014343, 7.0e-4 either way; 0.07 percent on the pair rate, below the statistical error of check 2 in R-low, 0.10 percent). The referee's caveat is kept in view (Codex 1, review of 2026-11-22, correction 4: a change of the optical depth is a change of physics): the three runs with the policy are realisations of inputs that differ by 7 parts in 10,000 in one rate, and are treated as realisations of one physics only at that level.
- **Why not the optical depth alone, and why not the temperature (facts from the toy ensemble, before any GPU time).** My first choice for the runs without the policy was the upstream temperature in its fourth digit (the momenta of the cold upstream plasma scale by 5e-4, the same random numbers). In the toy ensemble (CPU, 250 x 125 d_e, 120 time units) the three runs without the policy that differ only in that way are the same run to four digits (reconnection rate 0.051272, 0.051256, 0.051245; primaries at the end 2,720,160, 2,720,190, 2,719,850) and their sheet profiles correlate at +1.000 at all twelve outputs, while the three toy runs with the policy (optical depth 0.5, 0.4995, 0.5005) reach 0.89 to 0.95 by the end and scatter ten times more (rate: s = 1.5e-4 against 1.3e-5). A smooth, tiny change of every particle does not make a new realisation in the time available; a change of discrete particles does. On the GPU the optical depth alone is also weak at first: it changes which ions emit only for a random number that falls in a window of relative width 7e-4, i.e. about 10 pairs out of 14,000 by t = 300 and 60 out of 89,000 by t = 750, and (unlike on CPU threads, where one extra draw puts a whole thread out of step) each of the 221,184 generators of the card serves its own particles. The change of the layer in the toy (two runs, 3.003 and 2.997, against the unchanged run): correlation of the sheet profiles +0.98 after the first step, 0.79 and 0.80 at t = 10, 0.30 and 0.23 at t = 20, i.e. a new realisation of the layer from the start. On CPU threads this is helped by the same artefact (one changed draw puts a whole thread out of step), so the toy shows that the change is effective, not how fast the runs on the GPU separate.
- **Rejected alternatives:** the policy switched on with an optical depth that makes no pair (a new random stream, but then the run is not "without the policy"); the interval of particle clearing or any output option (they change the order of the arrays only, i.e. rounding, whose growth to a different plasmoid history is the slowest of all); rerunning the identical input (whether the atomic deposit on the GPU already gives another realisation is not known); a seed (a constant of the engine, `constant::RandomSeed`, upstream issue U11). An existence statement: an effective innocuous change exists without touching the engine, so the brief's stop "before these two" does not apply.
- **Not known beforehand (conjecture):** how long these differences take to grow into a different plasmoid history on the GPU. R-low and R-off, which differ from the first step by thousands of particles (the pairs) and by the random draws of the policy, agreed to a few percent until t of about 1100 and were unrelated by t = 1500. The toy runs on CPU threads overstate the speed (see above). This is why check 3 below carries an independence condition that can deny a verdict, and why its spectral window starts at t = 2000.

**Departures from the brief, with reasons.**

1. **`setup.t_open` is not written explicitly.** Fact: the generator reads it as a double (`pgens/reconnection/pgen.hpp:177-178`, `get<simtime_t>`; `simtime_t = double`, `src/global/global.h:383`), the engine stores every float of `[setup]` as `real_t` (`src/framework/parameters/parameters.cpp:323`), and in the single-precision build the run stops at start with "Bad any_cast for setup.t_open" (toy-size test on the Mac, kept in the evidence). Setting it would need a change of the generator, which the brief excludes for this stage. The default stays in force and is the value the brief asks for: 1.5 x half the extent in x = 750, as in R-off and R-low.
2. **The capacity of 1.6e8 is given to the four members too** (the brief names it for R-mid). A capacity is not physics, and a member that stopped for capacity could not be rerun under the brief (R-low peaked at 8.28e7 of 1.2e8 and was still rising).
3. **The members keep the particle output of the two lepton species** (the brief removes the ions): a few hundred MB, and it lets check 6 of a later stage use three realisations.
4. **R-low-b and R-low-c carry the change of the layer as well as the change of the optical depth that the brief names**, for the reason given above (on the GPU the optical depth alone changes about ten pairs in the first 300 time units). The two members b share one layer input and the two members c the other, so the two kinds are perturbed alike.

**Scripts.** Checks 1, 2, 4 and 5: the scripts of 97d78619, unchanged, through `run_checks.sh`. New, in `pgens/reconnection_bh/scripts/` of the stage-C branch: `check3_ensemble.py`, `check6_series.py`, `check6_health.py`; drivers in `stage-c/rusty/` (`run_c.sh`, `run_c.sbatch`, `analyze_c.sh`, `dryrun_c.sh`). Their SHA-256 and those of the five inputs are fixed in `SCRIPTS.sha256` and `INPUTS.sha256` before the first submission; the run driver refuses an input and the analysis refuses a script whose hash differs. Exercised before the runs: the drivers with stub engines on a login node (five cases of the stop flag, a changed input, an unknown name); the three new scripts on toy runs on the Mac (250 x 125 d_e, 120 time units: three without the policy, three with, and the toy run of stage A; the toy proves the pipeline and nothing about the physics): `check3_ensemble.py` with one of the runs standing in for R-mid, through its no-verdict branch (the toy runs without the policy are not independent, and the script says so) and, in a scratch copy with the independence bound lifted, through its three rules; `check6_series.py` on the toy run of stage A and on R-low itself; `check6_health.py` on the series of R-low against itself. One defect was found and fixed by these tests (the kind of a run was looked up by comparing run records). A toy copy of R-mid was still running on the Mac when the hashes were fixed; the first attempt at it stopped at start on the probability bound (0.1021 at optical depth 1.0 with the toy tables), which is the engine's own guard working.

**A run that stops early** is reported as such; its checks are evaluated on what was written and labelled partial, with no pass. The stops of the stage-B brief apply (a crash, a non-finite value, a capacity error, particles above the tables, pairs dropped, candidates refused by the loss bound, check 2 outside its tolerance, check 4 outside its criterion): report, no rerun with changed parameters.

**Stop flag of the runs (in the driver, not in the generator; the engine has none).** Every 60 s the driver reads the last line of `bh_counters.txt` and stops the run when, on two readings in a row, it shows a value that is not a number, particle-steps above the tables, pairs dropped for capacity, or a mean K of all leptons of the pairs above **175** with at least 1e5 of them present (ten times the largest value of R-low over its whole run, 17.5). The engine itself stops when a probability exceeds its bound (at this optical depth the probability reaches 0.083 at the upper edge of the tables, bound 0.1). The flag is a guard against an unmistakable runaway; the criteria are in check 6.

## 1. Checks 1, 2, 4 and 5 of R-mid: as registered for stage B

`run_checks.sh` of 97d78619 with R-mid in the place of R-low and the original R-off (job 7520718) as the run without the policy. The criteria are those of sections 1, 2, 4 and 5 of the stage-B pre-registration, word for word:

- **Check 1:** particle-steps above the tables 0; pairs dropped 0; every counter of R-off 0; refused candidates and particle-steps above the probability bound reported (expected 0: the loss bound cannot be reached below K = 15,019 at pair weight 1 whatever the optical depth).
- **Check 2:** |N_obs - N_pred| <= 4 sqrt(statistics^2 + stride^2 + cadence^2), no verdict if that tolerance exceeds 5 percent; N_pred from the saved ions (stride 5, every 20 time units) and the `bhref` table, with the optical depth of the input (0.14333). - **Check 4:** level(R-mid) <= 3 level(R-off) for the largest residual and for the root mean square. Reported besides: the same against R-off-b and R-off-c, and checks 1 and 4 of the members.
- **Check 5:** correlation and share at four times, expectations above 0.5 and above 0.9 (not criteria of the policy), spectra of the leptons.

## 3. Check 3, re-registered: the ensemble as the reference

Script `check3_ensemble.py`. Runs: three without the policy (R-off, R-off-b, R-off-c), three with (R-low, R-low-b, R-low-c), and R-mid.

**The ten scalars are those of stage B**, one number per run (the mean over its window of the value for the whole box): the reconnection rate over t >= 1000 (inflow speed in the four quadrants of the upstream region, from the field output); from the spectra over t >= 2000: mean K, the 99 and the 99.9 percent quantiles of K and the slope between K = 50 and 200, for the electrons and for the ions, and the fraction of the ions above threshold. The functions of `check3_compare.py` are used as they are.

**Independence (a condition, fixed here).** For every pair of runs and every field output: the correlation coefficient of the density of the primaries along the sheet (N_1 + N_2 averaged over |y| <= 20 d_e, 256 bins in x). Two runs count as independent realisations when the median of this correlation over the outputs with t >= 2000 is **below 0.5**. Calibration (fact, the two runs of stage B, which are known to differ): +0.66 at t = 700 (a common, mirror-symmetric shape before the boundaries open: the same number against the mirrored profile), then -0.11, -0.09, -0.14 at t = 1501, 2201, 2901. **If a pair of runs of the same kind is not independent, the scatter is not measured and check 3 gives no verdict.** Reported for every pair: the correlation at every output, and the last output at which it is at or above 0.5 (the time from which the two are apart).

**Scatter.** For every scalar: the standard deviation over the three runs of each kind, s_off and s_low (two degrees of freedom each), the pooled s_p = sqrt((s_off^2 + s_low^2) / 2) (four degrees of freedom, assuming the same scatter for both kinds), and s_new, the pooled standard deviation of the four new members only (two degrees of freedom, independent of the original pair).

**Rules.** Each holds for the ten scalars at once: Bonferroni, 0.5 percent per scalar two-sided for 5 percent over the family (Student t: 5.598 for four degrees of freedom, 14.089 for two). The bounds at the uncorrected 5 percent level (2.776 and 4.303) are printed beside them and a scalar outside only at that level is named, without a verdict.

- **A. The kind with the policy against the kind without:** |mean(low) - mean(off)| <= 5.598 s_p sqrt(2 / 3). Inside for all ten: no difference beyond the scatter. Outside: a difference beyond the scatter, to be understood (it is then the first evidence that pairs at one percent change the layer, or that the two kinds of perturbation are not equivalent).
- **B. The original pair (the question of the brief: was the 45 to 63 percent difference of stage B inside the scatter?):** |Q(R-low) - Q(R-off)| <= 14.089 s_new sqrt(2). Also printed, as the plain answer: the difference in units of s_new sqrt(2) and of s_p sqrt(2).
- **C. R-mid against the kind with the policy:** |Q(R-mid) - mean(low)| <= 5.598 s_p sqrt(4 / 3). Outside: an effect of the loading (physical or numerical), to be read together with check 6.

**Verdict of check 3:** "nothing outside the scatter", or "outside the scatter" with the list, or "no verdict" (independence). **What it can and cannot see.** With three runs of each kind the smallest difference rule A can detect is 4.6 s_p, and rule C 6.5 s_p: if the scatter is the 20 to 30 percent that the one pair of stage B suggests, only differences of order 100 percent would be detected. The purpose of this check is to measure the scatter; a pass means "no effect above this size", and the size is stated. Rule B has two degrees of freedom and is weak by construction; the number to read is the difference in units of the scatter. The rate's window starts at t = 1000, before the members are expected to be apart: its scatter can be underestimated, so the rate over t >= 2000 alone is reported beside it (no rule), with the number of primaries at the end, the pairs created and the largest ion K.

**Expectation (conjecture, written before the runs):** A and B inside for every scalar (the pairs of R-low hold 0.19 percent of the energy); C inside (in R-mid the pairs should be about 12 percent of the leptons and of order 2 percent of the energy).

## 6. Check 6 (new), R-mid: numerical health of the loaded plasma

Scripts `check6_series.py` and `check6_health.py`.

**The series.** At every field output (every 100 time units): the densest point of the pairs (N_3 + N_4 on blocks of 4 x 4 cells); in circles of radius 1, 2, 5, 10 and 20 d_e around it, the mean density of the pairs and of the primaries (N_1 + N_2) from the field output and, from the particle output nearest in time (stride 5), the median and mean K and the mean of 1 / gamma of the leptons of the pairs and of the ions (the primary electrons are not written and are taken to be like the ions, which have the same mass). Plasma frequency of the mixture in units of the upstream one, w^2 = sum over species of N_s <1 / gamma>_s, given as w_p dt and as cells per skin depth. Size of the plasmoid that holds the densest point: the equivalent radius of the connected set of blocks of 16 x 16 cells with N_1 + N_2 above 10 n0. Fewer than 20 written particles in a circle: no energy statistics there.

**Reference: the same series of R-low** (job 7520720), computed before this text was written (`stage-c/reference/r-low-7520720-core-series.npz`, SHA-256 `25bd7ff094c888c34704e072052b23bfc7cca18cad75e9126c532404991bfeaa`). Fact: from t = 1300 the densest point sits in one growing plasmoid; over its 14 outputs with a size of at least 25 d_e (t = 1601 to 2901, sizes 25.7 to 59.1 d_e) the leptons of the pairs inside 5 d_e have a median K of 1.085 to 1.490 and a mean of 1 / gamma of 0.4232 to 0.4674; inside 2 d_e, 1.472 to 2.472 and 0.3313 to 0.4101. In smaller plasmoids (t up to 1201) the densest pairs are few and hot (median K up to 17), which is why the criterion is restricted to large ones. Inside 1 d_e the statistics jump between two states (median K 1.0 to 4.6) and are reported only.

**Three criteria, all needed for a pass.**

- **6a, stability.** w_p dt of the mixture inside 5 d_e of the densest point is **below 2** at every field output (2 is the limit of the leapfrog scheme for plasma oscillations). Why 5 d_e and not 1: the currents are filtered 8 times, so an oscillation confined to the 4 cells of 1 d_e has its restoring force reduced (cos^16 of pi / 16 per dimension, 0.73), while one of 20 cells feels the full plasma frequency (0.99). Expected from R-low with ten times the pairs: at most 0.99 inside 5 d_e, 1.44 inside 2 d_e, 2.29 inside 1 d_e. The values inside 1, 2, 10 and 20 d_e are reported, with the number of outputs at or above 2.
- **6b, no heating of the pairs where they are densest.** At every field output of R-mid at which the plasmoid holding the densest point has a size of at least 25 d_e: the median K of the leptons of the pairs is at most **2.980** inside 5 d_e and **4.943** inside 2 d_e (twice the largest value of the reference), and their mean of 1 / gamma is at least **0.2116** inside 5 d_e and **0.1656** inside 2 d_e (half the smallest value of the reference). No such output: no verdict. An output whose plasmoid is larger than 1.5 times the largest of the reference (88.6 d_e) is reported, not judged. Why the same values are expected if the plasma is healthy: the birth spectrum does not depend on the optical depth, and the pairs carry little of the pressure even where they outnumber the primaries (inside 5 d_e the primaries have a mean K of 69 to 90). Why a factor 2: the reference varies by 20 percent around its mean over 1300 time units, and a halving of 1 / gamma is the size of change that matters for the plasma frequency.
- **6c, the box.** The largest value over t >= 300 (with at least 1000 leptons present) of the mean K of all leptons of the pairs, from `bh_counters.txt` (every 10 steps), is at most **twice the largest such value over R-low, R-low-b and R-low-c** (R-low: 17.5 at t = 1372). At t = 2901 in R-low a third of the leptons written sit inside 5 d_e of the densest point and four fifths inside 20 d_e, so heating of the core would show in this number.

**Verdict of check 6:** pass if 6a, 6b and 6c pass; fail if one fails; no verdict if 6b has no output of that size and nothing fails. **Reported without criterion:** pairs per primary in the circles and in the box against time (whether the cores become pair-dominated); the mean K and the mean of 1 / gamma of the ions in the circles against the band of the reference (69.2 to 89.7 and 0.0926 to 0.1167 inside 5 d_e); cells per skin depth. **What this check can and cannot see.** A heating of the pairs by less than a factor 2 is not flagged. The reference is one plasmoid of one realisation; a failure of 6b or 6c is a signal to look (numerical heating, or a real effect of pairs that are no longer passive), not a proven defect, and it does not by itself say which. The check does not test energy conservation (the box is open) and does not see the primary electrons.
